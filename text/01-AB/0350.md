:::entry Angle
Angle (æ·ŋg'l), sb.² Also 4-5 aungel, 4-7 angel, 5-6 -ule, -yll. [a. Fr. angle:—L. angul-um (nom. -us) corner, a dim. form, of which the prim. *angus is not in L.; cf. Gr. ἄγκος a bend, a hollow angle, and L. ang-ĕre to compress in a bend or fold, to strangle; Aryan root ank- to bend.]
1. The indefinite space included between two meeting lines or planes, the shape of which depends upon their mutual inclination; hence in Geom. the degree of inclination of two lines to each other, or of one line to a horizontal or vertical base-line.
The angle is measured by the portion of the circumference of a circle described from the intersection of the lines as centre, which is intercepted between the two lines. The inclination of two lines in the same plane is a plane angle, which may be rectilineal or curvilineal, as it is formed by straight or curved lines; the inclination of two lines on the surface of a sphere is a spherical angle; the space included by more than two plane angles meeting at a point is a solid angle.
