:::entry Academical
Academical (ækăde·mikăl), a. and sb. [f. prec. + -AL¹.]
A. adj.
1. = ACADEMIC A 1. rare.
1666 J. SMITH Old Age 256  With Devotion to admire that Academical Inscription ἀγνώστῳ θεῷ [to an unknown God].

:::entry Academy
Academy (ăkæ·dĕmi), also 5 achadomye, 6 achademya. [a. Fr. académie, ad. L. acadēmia, a. Gr. ἀκαδημία, more properly ἀκαδήμεια adj., f. Ἀκάδημος name of a man ; cf. Horace's silvas Academi, the 'groves of Academus.']

:::entry -acal
-acal, compound suffix ; consisting of -AL repr. L. -ālis, -āle 'of the nature of, belonging to,' added to -AC (q.v.), which although strictly an adj. ending was so often used substantively, e.g. demoniac, maniac, ammoniac, aphrodisiac, that it became usual to make the adj. in -acal even when no sb. occurs, as heliacal. As in the cogn. -IC, -ICAL, adjectives in -ac are primary objective attributes, of or pertaining to the thing, while adjectives in -acal are only secondary, of the nature of or connected with the attribute in -ac, or its embodiment, hence more remotely and subjectively relating to the thing ; e.g. the cardiac arteries, a cardiac (medicine), cardi-acal qualities of a herb. But this distinction is not always observed. Examples : ammoniacal, aphrodisiacal, cardiacal, demoniacal, heliacal, hypochondriacal, maniacal, paradisiacal, prosodiacal, theriacal.
